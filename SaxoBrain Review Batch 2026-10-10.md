## 1. Alex Shapiro: Of Wind and Touch

[Open your work file](</Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/content/works/Alex Shapiro - Of Wind and Touch.md>)

**Identity and scope:** Shapiro identifies this as her own adaptation of the bassoon-and-piano work *Of Breath and Touch*. Brandon Masterman commissioned the baritone version in 2006. It lasts approximately ten minutes in one movement. This is evidence of a deliberate concert adaptation for saxophone. The source provides no evidence that it was simplified for young players. [Composer's work page](https://www.alexshapiro.org/OfWindandTouchpg1.html).

**Score observations:** the [first page](https://www.alexshapiro.org/First%20page%20gifs/OfWindandTouchpg1.jpg) is explicitly labeled baritone saxophone in E-flat. It begins at quarter note = 92+, with changing meters and high, sustained phrases over a repeating piano figure. [Page 4](https://www.alexshapiro.org/First%20page%20gifs/OfWindandTouchpg4.jpg) contrasts those phrases with a secco passage, which calls for a dry, detached character. It includes sixteenth-note figures, accidentals, and meter changes.

**Review focus, inferred from those pages:** upper-register control, breath planning, contrasting articulation, and reliable coordination across meter changes. These are excerpt observations. They do not establish the work's complete written range.

**Listen:** [Masterman and Henry Orazi, composer-hosted baritone excerpt](https://www.alexshapiro.org/RealAudio/OfWindandTouchpartial.mp3). The work page separately identifies its video as the bassoon original. Your current streaming field points to a publisher product page.

**Gap:** the complete perusal requires a composer-provided access code. The two public excerpts are the score evidence used here.

## 3. Anders Hillborg: Plainsong and Echoes

[Open your work file](</Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/content/works/Anders Hillborg - Plainsong And Echoes.md>)

**Identity:** the original version is soprano saxophone, alternatively oboe, with **four cellos**. Faber gives 2013 and eight minutes. It specifically requires a reverberant auditorium, such as a church. The premiere used Theo Hillborg and Quattro Ragazzi. [Publisher's work page](https://www.fabermusic.com/music/plainsong-and-echoes).

**Score observations:** the [public perusal](https://www.fabermusic.com/music/plainsong-and-echoes/score) is in concert pitch. Printed pages 1, 2, and 4 were inspected. The opening is approximately quarter note = 80, with sustained slurred lines, fermatas, and changes among 4/4, 5/4, and 6/4. The cello texture becomes more independent on page 2. Page 4 holds the saxophone line over many measures while the cellists enter with repeated articulated figures.

**Review focus, inferred from the inspected pages:** sustained sound, intonation against the cellos, pacing long phrases, and ensemble entrances through shifting meters. These pages provide little basis for judging rapid saxophone technique. Slow surface rhythm alone cannot establish a level.

**Collection fit:** original concert chamber music with distinctive ensemble and venue requirements. A [separate strings version](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/work/plainsong-and-echoes-version-for-soprano-sax-and-strings) also exists. Its instrumentation should be distinguished from this four-cello edition.

**Gap:** complete transposed soprano range and a matching recording remain unverified. The existing Cello tag does not communicate the number of players.

## 4. An-Lun Huang: Chinese Rhapsody No. 3

[Open your work file](</Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/content/works/An-Lun Huang - Chinese Rhapsody No. 3.md>)

**College and professional relevance:** Xinyu Zhang programmed the complete five-movement work on a 2023 Doctor of Musical Arts recital at the University of British Columbia. The program lists Lento, Andante, Allegro, Adagio, and Presto. Its notes identify 1988 and dedicate the work to Paul Brodie. [University recital program, pages 1 and 3](https://music.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2023/03/apr14_xinyu_zhang.pdf).

**Musical evidence:** Yue Lu's 2022 doctoral dissertation at the University of North Carolina at Greensboro is devoted to the work's interpretation. The university abstract emphasizes Chinese musical style and imitation of traditional instrument timbres. Search-indexed dissertation text specifies soprano saxophone for movements 4 and 5 and discusses accelerated grace notes. [University record and abstract](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/listing.aspx?id=37900) · [Dissertation](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/uncg/f/Lu_uncg_0154D_13478.pdf).

**Corroboration and listening route:** saxophonist Chen Wang's own recording page describes five continuous movements using both alto and soprano. It hosts a live recording. [Performer recording page](https://www.chenwang.art/recording).

**Review focus, inferred from this evidence:** instrument switching, five contrasting sections, and idiomatic ornamentation and timbre. The doctoral programming provides concrete evidence that this work belongs within your collection's focus. It supplies no automatic undergraduate-year placement.

**Data issue:** your entry currently lists alto and piano only. Confirm the alto-to-soprano change against the Dorn edition before updating tags. The Canadian Music Centre dates its [string-orchestra version](https://collections.cmccanada.org/final/ViewRecord.aspx?record=c228ebb3-fefe-4070-b55a-130968f1e620&template=LibCol) to 1989. Keep that version's date separate from the 1988 piano version.

**Gap:** direct dissertation download timed out. The abstract, indexed excerpts, and recital program were available. Full score, exact range, and detailed technique requirements remain unverified.

## 6. Amy Williams: Duo

**Musical evidence:** Williams identifies a 2011, approximately eight-minute work for alto and piano in three movements: converge, merge, diverge. She describes the first as juxtaposing diverse material in progressively shorter phrases. The second uses a complementary relationship between the instruments. In the third, intertwined parts gradually become independent. [Composer's works page, Duo entry](https://amywilliamsmusic.com/works/).

The composer also links [a Duo recording on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/5fZJcXQoDGrLOVi6DEs9HD).

**Review focus, inferred from the composer's description:** handling abrupt changes of material and maintaining ensemble coordination as the parts become independent. These are structural concerns. They do not establish specific rhythms, range, or extended techniques.

**Collection fit:** commissioned original concert repertoire with an identified professional recording. No evidence of a young-player transcription was found.

**Gap:** no readable score or exact purchase edition was verified. Listening to the three movements is the available next basis for judgment. Your stored streaming link is a general composer catalog page.

[Official repertoire list, printed page 30 / PDF page 31](https://lcme.uwl.ac.uk/media/rzjfqioh/woodwind-diplomas-repertoire-list.pdf#page=31).

**Edition limitation:** the diploma list does not print RV 45 or an arranger. The number and key correspond to your title, but the exact saxophone edition match remains unconfirmed.

**Collection flag:** retain for further edition review. The diploma evidence provides a reason to consider this transcription for advanced study. No evidence was found that your edition is a simplified lyrical excerpt for young players.

**Review focus, inferred from the original and syllabus:** stylistic articulation, ornamentation, and contrast between slow and fast movements. Exact saxophone range, technical adaptation, and accompaniment remain unknown. Your entry lacks an arranger and accompaniment tag.

**Edition confirmed:** Elkin Music's distributor catalog identifies Robert Muller's arrangement of the bass-clarinet-and-orchestra *Romanze* for bassoon, trombone, alto saxophone, or baritone saxophone with piano, Spratt1262. The arranger's intention to target young saxophonists has **not** been established. Whether it remains useful for college tone, phrasing, or recital programming is your call.

**Review focus, inferred from the source analysis:** tone, phrase shaping, and expressive recitative. The original bass-clarinet range cannot be used as the saxophone arrangement's written range.

**Gap:** no readable Muller saxophone part or matching saxophone performance was verified. The association page contains conflicting dates and measure counts, so neither is used here.
