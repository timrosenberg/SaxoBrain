The following is a list of ligatures that are upgrades over the standard "Rovner" model that most players begin with.

⭐ = ligature Dr. Rosenberg currently uses

# [Vandoren Optimum](https://www.amazon.com/Vandoren-LC07P-Ligature-Saxophone-Interchangeable/dp/B000BAQS3C/ref=sr_1_1?tag=timothyrosenb-20)

- Nice single-screw mechanism for tightening.
- Comes with three inter-changeable plates for different sound qualities.

![vandoren optimum.jpg](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/vandoren_optimum.jpg)

# [Vandoren Optimum M/O](https://www.amazon.com/Vandoren-LC57DP-Ligature-Plastic-Saxophone/dp/B005DOJV94/ref=sr_1_1?tag=timothyrosenb-20)

- Similar to the Optimum; but, *a lot* lighter. (Mass of ligature can effect the tone and response slightly.)
- Comes in a few different platings. (Platings can effect the tone slightly.)

![](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/51nB1NvglsL._AC_SL1000_.jpg)

# [BG Tradition](https://www.prowinds.com/product/BGL11/BG)

- A classic design that has been popular for decades.
- Available in gold and silver plating.
- ⭐ Baritone
    
    ![bg2.png](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/bg2.png)
    

# [D'addario / Hartman H-Ligature](https://www.daddario.com/products/woodwinds/alto-saxophone/alto-sax-ligatures-and-caps/alto-h-ligature/)

- A two-screw design that was popular in the 1960s-90s; but, fell out of favor due to the original design’s durability issues.
- D’addario revived the design with stronger joints at the common failure points and more consistent quality.
- Available in silver and gold plating.
    
    ![HLigature Cap Alto Sax.webp](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/HLigature_Cap_Alto_Sax.webp)
    

# [Ishimori (Woodstone) Metal Ligature](https://www.meridianwinds.com/shop/c/p/Ishimori-Saxophone-Ligatures-for-Selmer-Rubber-Mouthpieces-x56220532.htm)

- Several finishes available. Most classical players prefer the solid silver model.
    - Very hard to buy in the USA.
- Gold plated model is similar; but, exactly the same as solid silver. However, it’s easier to find in the USA.
- All platings are *incredibly* expensive.
- If you believe that all ligatures are the same, you haven’t tried an Ishimori. There is a noticeable difference (in a good way, I think) between other ligatures and an Ishimori. And there is also a small; but, perceptible difference between the different platings. I don’t know how this can be possible; but, I am not ruling out magic.
- ⭐ Alto
    
    ![silver_20210405081649.jpg](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/silver_20210405081649.jpg)
    

# [BG Duo](https://www.prowinds.com/product/BGLD/BG?srsltid=AfmBOor5gnsnmKqudeAiV7_73aDh-yDvrEkNsZGJwtAOagg1rsDsvZAY)

- I like this model specifically on a Vandoren tenor saxophone hard-rubber mouthpiece. (T20, TL3, TL5) It is available for soprano and alto mouthpieces too. Comes in gold plated, silver plated, and rose gold plated finishes.
- It is different from the [[Saxophone Ligatures|Tradition]] in the way it makes contact with the mouthpiece. It only touches the mouthpiece in four small spots, which does *something* to the way the reed responds.
- It is a “poor person’s” Ishimori. (I mean that in the least offensive way possible.) It’s not *exactly* the same; but, it’s about 85% the same at a much lower price. If you don’t play classical tenor a lot and you don’t want to shell out the big-bucks for an Ishimori, this will work well for you.
- ⭐ Tenor
    
    ![duo_plaquee_argent_ld_20231101151845.jpg](/media/resources/equipment/saxophone-ligatures/duo_plaquee_argent_ld_20231101151845.jpg)
    

# [Silverstein CRYO4](https://www.silversteinworks.com/product/cryo4-saxophone-ligature-gen-5/)

- This model works well when using synthetic reeds, such as Légère. It offers more levels of tightening, which keeps the synthetic reed from sliding on the table of the mouthpiece.
- *Unbelievably expensive* for what it is. Look for a used one.
- Ignore all of the hype in the marketing materials from Silverstein. It’s just a string ligature with a screw. I don’t notice a difference between models (remember that I’m the guy who can hear a difference between *platings* of Ishimori ligatures).
- I choose the CRYO4 because I like the stability of five loops of cord rather than the four loops on the [Quattro](https://www.silversteinworks.com/product/quattro-saxophone-ligature-gen-5/). The [Original](https://www.silversteinworks.com/product/original-saxophone-ligature-gen-5/) model is fine; but, two sets of adjusters on the CRYO4 give you a little more fine-tuning ability for the “grip”. The plating makes no difference.
- ⭐ Soprano
