### Research Review, October 11, 2026

[[SaxoBrain - Review and Repair Record]]

Catalog entry: [[Wolfgang Jacobi - Sonata]].

Wolfgang Jacobi’s Sonata for E-flat alto saxophone and piano was written in 1930/31 for Sigurd Rascher; the 1965 Bourne edition has three movements. UNT dissertation pages 40–42 (PDF pp. 48–50) reproduce examples from the saxophone/piano score and analyze their technical demands.
[Wolfgang Jacobi official site](https://www.wolfgang-jacobi.de/en/works/) (opened): Composer’s official works page identifies Jacobi’s early-1930s saxophone sonata and notes Rascher’s performances.
[Bayerisches Musiker-Lexikon Online](https://www.bmlo.lmu.de/4001036) (opened): Bayerisches Musiker-Lexikon Online record identifies the work’s dedication to Sigurd Rascher, 1931 date, 10:30 duration, Bourne 1965 publication, and its own difficulty rating.
[UNT dissertation, annotated repertoire entry and reproduced score figures](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc4959/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) (score_inspected, printed pp. 40–42; PDF pp. 48–50): Visual inspection of the UNT dissertation bibliography and examples identifies movements Allegro ma non troppo, Sarabande, Allegro and reproduces score figures for movement II, measures 6–7 and 47–49. Its analysis describes angular sixteenth-note passages, syncopated piano writing, four through seven subdivisions of the quarter-note pulse, and sudden dynamic changes. The figures are excerpts, not the full score.

Score observations:
- The reproduced second-movement example at measures 6–7 visibly labels quintuplet and sextuplet groupings, while the dissertation’s accompanying analysis says four, five, six, and seven subdivisions of the quarter-note pulse occur within two measures. The later example (measures 47–49) shows sustained saxophone tones with a rapid ornamental run, contrasting crescendos/decrescendos and a soft ending over active piano writing. These are reproduced excerpts, not a complete-part survey. ([score source](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc4959/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf), printed pp. 41–42; PDF pp. 49–50)

Collection/transcription context: Longstanding recital sonata written for Rascher and discussed in a scholarly annotated bibliography; the bibliography’s difficulty characterization is source terminology, not a SaxoBrain level.

Review focus: Inference from the reproduced examples: check rhythmic subdivision and ensemble alignment in the Sarabande, then compare the first and third movements’ angular sixteenths and the soft sustained endings for tone and intonation control.

Open questions: Only dissertation excerpts and analytical discussion were inspected, not the full saxophone part; complete written range and movement-wide demands remain unknown. No college-year mapping is assigned from the dissertation bibliography.
