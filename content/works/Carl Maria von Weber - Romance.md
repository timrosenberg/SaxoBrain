### Research Review, October 11, 2026

[[Review 0034 - Carl Maria von Weber - Romance|Full review note]]

Catalog entry: [[Carl Maria von Weber - Romance]].

The catalog’s alto saxophone/piano edition is Marc Reift EMR2296E, as identified by retailer search results. The attribution to Carl Maria von Weber is highly doubtful: Weber died in 1826, while the work first appeared in 1890 for euphonium, an instrument developed later; the scholarly Weber edition retains it only among doubtful attributions.
[Carl-Maria-von-Weber-Gesamtausgabe, “Die Romanza appassionata – eine Komposition von Weber?”](https://weber-gesamtausgabe.de/de/A009005/Aktuelles/A050203.html) (opened): Scholarly Weber-Gesamtausgabe research reports the first known publication in 1890 for euphonium and concludes Weber authorship is highly improbable; later editions appeared for cello, bassoon, trombone, and viola.
[Musikforum Demond](https://www.demond.de/weber-c-m-von-romance-alto-saxophone-klavier-470683) (search_index_only): Retail listing identifies Marc Reift EMR2296E for alto saxophone and piano; the product page itself is not opened.
[Carl-Maria-von-Weber-Gesamtausgabe](https://weber-gesamtausgabe.de/de/A009005/Aktuelles/A050203.html) (opened): Scholarly source explains that this piece first appeared in 1890 for euphonium, after Weber’s death, and argues the attribution is highly improbable; it remains under doubtful attribution in the Weber catalogue.

Collection/transcription context: Later saxophone arrangement of a work with doubtful Weber attribution. Historical identity and saxophone score require confirmation.

Review focus: Inference: confirm that the saxophone edition derives from the same late-19th-century Romanza appassionata before judging its musical demands; Weber attribution should not be treated as settled.

Open questions: Exact author and source version remain uncertain. No saxophone score, recording, or programming evidence was verified.
