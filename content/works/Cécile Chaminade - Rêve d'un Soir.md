### Research Review, October 11, 2026

[[Review 0060 - Cécile Chaminade - Rêve d'un Soir|Full review note]]

Catalog: [Cécile Chaminade - Rêve d'un Soir.md](/Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/content/works/Cécile Chaminade - Rêve d'un Soir.md). Full evidence log: [research record](/Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/research-review-2026-10-11/lane-3/records/0060.json).

**Identity and findings.** The cataloged soprano-saxophone item is a transcription of Cécile Chaminade’s song Rêve d’un soir, originally for voice and piano and first published in 1891. IMSLP and the University of Rochester’s Sibley Music Library provide the source song score, but not the cataloged saxophone arrangement.

**Sources checked.**
- [IMSLP, original song record](https://imslp.org/wiki/R%C3%AAve_d%27un_soir_%28Chaminade%2C_C%C3%A9cile%29) (opened): IMSLP identifies the original as a voice-and-piano song, first published 1891, and hosts high- and low-voice scores. No saxophone arrangement appears in its arrangements list.
- [University of Rochester, Sibley Music Library](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/institutionalPublicationPublicView.action?institutionalItemId=16191&versionNumber=1) (opened): University of Rochester’s Sibley Music Library catalog describes the 1892 Schirmer volume Twenty Melodies with French and English Words, including Rêve d’un soir, as voice and piano.
- [East Carolina University catalog record](https://catalog.lib.ecu.edu/catalog/580822) (search_index_only): A saxophone-journal recording catalog lead credits Frank Bongiorno and pianist Barry Salwen with a soprano-saxophone version and playalong; this confirms a recording lead but not a freely playable, matched edition.

- [Institutional record for the source vocal collection; does not supply the saxophone arrangement.](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/institutionalPublicationPublicView.action?institutionalItemId=16191&versionNumber=1).

**Collection and audience context.** Transcription of a vocal art song originally for voice and piano. Its basic source texture is a lyrical vocal melody, but the saxophone arrangement’s edits, range, and technical demands were not inspected; preservation of that melody is a working inference, not verified score evidence.
