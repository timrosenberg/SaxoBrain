These are retained as differing source statements, with no composition date filled.

Score observations: Visually inspected Wehage’s public opening score page 2 and later score page 5, mm.43-51. These are excerpts of a separate edition, not RON1164 or the original Sax print. The opening score uses common time and Introduction: Allegro. The E-flat alto line has no key signature, against three flats in piano. This is written saxophone notation, not concert-pitch saxophone scoring. Opening saxophone line moves between sustained tones, high-register entries and slurred chromatic runs, with accents and detached repeated notes. Piano initially doubles the contour over repeated bass/chord patterns, then develops more independent treble figures. Later page 5 shows nearly continuous slurred sixteenth-note scalar figures with many accidentals and register changes. Piano has repeated chordal attacks, dolce melodic motion and sustained harmonies. Inference: the solo line needs sustained finger coordination and breath planning through running phrases. The two inspected pages cannot establish full range, every tempo change or all editorial differences between Wehage and Ronkin.

Collection context: Original nineteenth-century alto/piano contest work with documented youth competition and university faculty performance. Wehage’s actual saxophone notation shows extended chromatic running passages; no exact youth simplification verified.

Open questions: Ronkin RON1164 and original Adolphe Sax notation remain uninspected. Full written range and edition-specific editorial differences unknown.
