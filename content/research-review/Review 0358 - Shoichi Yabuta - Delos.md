**Identity and findings.** Shoichi Yabuta’s Delos is a seven-movement alto saxophone and piano work in Zen-On’s Sugawa Saxophone Collection, SEO-030 / product 560030. The score preface gives a duration of about 13 minutes, commission by Nobuya Sugawa, and 2021 premiere by Sugawa and pianist Minako Koyanagi.

**Sources checked.**
- [European American Music Corporation](https://www.eamdc.com/news/zen-on-releases-two-new-publications-of-music-by-shoichi-yabuta-and-jun-nagao/) (opened): EAMDC's publisher announcement documents the commission, premiere, and contrasting movement textures.
- [Presto Music](https://www.prestomusic.com/sheet-music/products/9317783--yabuta-s-delos) (opened): Zen-On retailer listing gives the publication details and page count.
- [Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/49047143/delos) (opened): Zen-On product record lists 44 pages.
- [Zen-On official public Delos score trial, product 560030](https://www.zen-on.co.jp/trial/560030/HTML5/pc.html#/page/5) (score_inspected, viewer pp.3-8; printed score pp.4-7): Zen-On’s public 34-page ActiBook sample for product 560030 was visually inspected, including preface pages 3-4 and score pages 5-8. The preface describes seven contrasting movements and identifies the saxophone/piano roles; the score opening shows an active, slurred alto line over strongly accented piano sonorities, and a later sampled passage contains denser flowing saxophone figures with sustained/slurred groupings and changing dynamics. These observations apply only to the pages seen.

**Score observations.**
- The publisher preface identifies seven movements with marked contrasts, including a piano solo, a lyrical saxophone movement, a movement built around held saxophone notes, a rhythmic movement, and a finale derived from movement I. In the inspected opening score page, the alto saxophone line uses active slurred figures while the piano has strongly accented chordal writing and very low-register material. A later inspected page includes denser flowing saxophone figures, sustained/slurred groups, and changing dynamics. No complete range claim is made.

**Review focus.** Inference from the publisher preface and inspected excerpts: assess movement-to-movement contrast, coordination across solo/lyrical/rhythmic textures, and control of the active slurred line against the piano’s strongly articulated accompaniment.
