## Chat batch 11 follow-up, 2026-10-14

Actual AL21654 cover and Hal Leonard’s exact-edition record confirm alto saxophone and piano and the ballet-excerpt identity. The cover gives MCMLVI, or 1956. The official Leduc/Ibert catalog dates its AL21654 saxophone-and-piano entry 1931, while listing Le Chevalier errant and its four-movement symphonic concert version as 1935. Groth’s descriptive text says ballet completed 1936 and piano version 1956. The dates conflict at the precise excerpt/original level, so composed remains blank. The Philharmonie de Paris guide’s indexed entry describes legato sixteenth-note phrases and no detached articulation, with a 2:30 timing. The actual guide PDF did not open in bounded web and curl attempts. That timing differs from exact recordings, including Wonki Lee and Esther Lim at 4:52 and Sergey Kolesov with Andrey Shibko at 5:18. Do not state 2:30 as a verified expected duration. The official catalog documents a professional recording by Arno Bornkamp and S. Grotenhuis, Olympia OCD469. Current public YouTube metadata independently identifies Wonki Lee and Esther Lim’s Air recording, released August 6, 2021. The Dutch KNMO woodwind solo repertory lists Leduc’s alto L’Age d’Or for competition use. No explicit young-player simplification was verified.

Score observations: Two small publicly embedded crops of the orchestral saxophone line were inspected, not the AL21654 saxophone-and-piano score. The first is labeled rehearsal 32, Calmo ed espressivo, with long slurs, tied notes, short beamed figures and accidentals. The second orchestral crop is labeled rehearsal 34, Tempo, and shows an alto saxophone line of continuous sixteenth-note figures grouped under long slurs, starting p. These crops support the source movement’s connected figuration, not the full piano edition’s range or precise notation. Written-pitch transposition of the cropped orchestral score is not established. No pitch-range claim is transferred from these source crops to AL21654. Teaching inference is limited to maintaining an even connected line across sustained phrases and flowing figuration.

Collection context: Ballet excerpt with original alto saxophone in the orchestral source. Professional recording and competition repertoire documented. No young-player simplification established.
