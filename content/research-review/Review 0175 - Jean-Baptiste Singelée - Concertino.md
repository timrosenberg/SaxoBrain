## Chat batch 11 follow-up, 2026-10-14

Exact-edition conflict: Groth’s linked RON1125 record credits Bruce Ronkin. The existing Jean-Marie Londeix edition attribution is not verified for that purchase. The public N1125 preview is one opening score page and carries Roncorp’s 2005 copyright. It must not supply a composition year. The Concertino was performed at the 1861 Conservatoire competition, according to the BnF scan’s cataloged title. IMSLP gives composition as 1861 or before, which is not an exact year. Concrete facilitation: the catalog-linked Roncorp preview prints an ossia marked facilité above the m.15 area. It replaces the main line’s displaced entrance and more complex short-note figure with continuous eighth-note motion. This proves an easier alternative exists inside this edition, without proving the entire work was simplified. L’Intèrpret’s 2024/25 school admission list includes Concertino (Singelée) in E-flat alto repertoire for entry to third year Grau professional. The list omits opus and editor; linkage to Op.78 is an inference from the composer/title/scoring match. A named alto/piano recording by Levente Puskás and Judit Váradi was recorded at the University of Debrecen Faculty of Music, approximately 3:16. This documents university-associated performance, without establishing the edition used.

Score observations: Visually inspected the entire one-page N1125 Roncorp preview, plus pages 1 and 3 of Jesús Galeano’s separate three-page alto part. Galeano’s notation is distinguished from the purchased Ronkin edition. Roncorp opening: common time, Allegro Moderato, one-flat written E-flat alto staff against four flats in piano. The saxophone opening is explicitly labeled (piano), while Solo/Risoluto begins at m.9. Do not interpret the initial cue as a compulsory solo entrance. The main solo opening uses slurred sixteenth-note scalar runs, chromatic accidentals, tied notes, crescendos and register changes. Piano accompaniment alternates melody doubled in the introduction with offbeat chords and sustained bass under the solo. The printed facilité ossia at m.15 is the strongest direct evidence of teaching accommodation found. Its existence does not erase the primary part’s technical demands. Galeano part page 3, mm.61-89, shows further chromatic sixteenth runs, sixteenth-note triplet figures, contrasting léger/legato articulation, rallentando/Tempo returns and a final ff cadence. This passage evidence applies to Galeano’s edition; Ronkin’s later pages were unavailable. No complete range is claimed.

Collection context: Original saxophone contest work also documented in school admission repertoire and a university-associated recording. Roncorp includes a specific easier ossia; no general difficulty assignment follows.

Open questions: Ronkin full score and entire written range not inspected. Londeix credit in existing catalog conflicts with the linked Ronkin edition and needs Tim’s review. Adolphe Sax is historical publisher, while the linked current edition is Roncorp/Northeastern; preserve existing publisher pending review. No exact composition year established. Galeano part is freely accessible but is not a full score, so download stays blank.
