## Chat batch 12 follow-up, 2026-10-14

The inspected edition is the original orchestral concerto in Walter S. Hartley’s piano reduction, supplied with a solo alto saxophone part. Its manuscript heading gives Worley’s date 1962; the printed footer gives a separate 2001 Elkan-Vogel copyright. The 1962 date is therefore supported directly by the score rather than inferred from publication. Groth’s exact listing gives Moderato, Adagio, and Molto Allegro. The orchestral recording calls the first movement Allegro moderato. These movement labels come from their respective sources and should not be silently normalized. Professional performance is documented by Albany’s recording with Lawrence Gwozdz, alto saxophone, the Bohuslav Martinu Philharmonic and conductor Kirk Trevor. Juan Daniel Oviedo-Reina’s own video description documents movement III with Darío Martin, piano, at a Fall 2020 saxophone studio jury in the University of Southern Mississippi School of Music. No youth teaching adaptation or simplification was established. Groth counts 64 pages for the supplied score and part. Schott lists 16 pages for the separate solo part 164-00254P and an approximate playing time of 21:30. Page counts are not evidence of musical difficulty.

Score observations: Inspected the cover and one opening piano-reduction page. The title explicitly identifies saxophone and orchestra and piano reduction by Walter S. Hartley. The saxophone staff is labeled E-flat alto saxophone and is transposed; its key signature differs from the piano. This is only the first movement’s opening, not a complete solo-part inspection. The opening is Moderato, quarter note 112–120, initially in common time. After orchestral material in the piano and several solo rests, the saxophone enters at mf. The visible solo then moves into 5/4 with slurred beamed groups, staccato notes, accidentals, a crescendo, and a leap to a pitch several ledger lines above the staff. A change to common time and later return to 5/4 are visible. The piano reduction includes chromatic chords, sustained bass notes, accented repeated chords and beamed runs. The sampled opening establishes changing-meter ensemble coordination, chromatic fingering and upper-register control. The full range, later cadenzas and remaining two movements were not inspected.

Collection context: Original concerto with orchestral scoring and piano-reduction edition; verified professional recording and university jury excerpt. No youth simplification documented.

Open questions: Only one first-movement notation page was inspected. Full written range and later movement demands remain unverified. The recordings were verified by metadata without audition. The commercial recording uses orchestra, while the university video covers movement III with piano only. The Library of Congress archive finding-aid search names 164-00254, but no exact opened score catalog record was verified for a library-score link.
