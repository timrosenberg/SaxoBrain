## Research

Catalog: [Daniel Schnyder - Sonata.md](/Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/content/works/Daniel Schnyder - Sonata.md). Full evidence log: [research record](/Users/trosenberg/Documents/GitHub/SaxoBrain/research-review-2026-10-11/lane-3/records/0063.json).

**Identity and findings.** Daniel Schnyder’s Sonata for Soprano Saxophone and Piano is the soprano work in Edition Kunzelmann GM1725A, issued as a 36-page score and part. Carlos Felipe Vina’s 2015 University of Miami DMA guide concerns the composer’s subsequent bassoon adaptation; it reports the bassoon version’s multiphonics and Latin-jazz/Balkan influences, but those instrument-specific analysis details should not be transferred to the soprano score.

**Sources checked.**
- [Presto Music edition record](https://www.prestomusic.com/sheet-music/products/8188273--schnyder-daniel-sonate-fur-sopransax-und-klavier) (opened): Edition record identifies soprano saxophone and piano, Kunzelmann GM1725A, 36 pages.
- [Daniel Schnyder composer site](https://www.danielschnyder.com/sheet_music/list_of_compositions_2001.pdf) (search_index_only): Composer’s work list mentions the soprano saxophone sonata as recorded by Daniel Schnyder and Kenny Drew Jr.
- [Carlos Felipe Vina, University of Miami DMA dissertation (bassoon version)](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/A-Performance-Guide-of-Daniel-Schnyders/991031447816902976) (opened): The 2015 University of Miami DMA dissertation is a performance guide to Schnyder’s bassoon-and-piano version. Its abstract says the composer adapted the sonata from the original soprano saxophone version in collaboration with Martin Kuuskmann; technical discussion is about the bassoon version.
- [Sheet Music Plus preview of Edition Kunzelmann](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/sonata-for-soprano-saxophone-and-piano-20784285.html) (score_inspected, score preview, p.1): Page preview visibly shows the soprano saxophone and piano score, first movement marked q=112, with a slurred saxophone line above a rhythmically active piano part. Product details identify Edition Kunzelmann GM-1725A and 36 pages.

**Score observations.**
- In the publisher/retailer preview’s first score page, the soprano part begins at quarter=112 with a slurred, moving melodic line over an active piano part with repeating rhythmic figures and chord changes. This is a limited opening-page sample, not a complete technical survey.

**Score/sample.**
- [Edition Kunzelmann product page with visible page-1 score preview and edition details.](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/sonata-for-soprano-saxophone-and-piano-20784285.html) (inspected).

**Listening.**
- [Composer lists a recording by Daniel Schnyder and Kenny Drew Jr.](https://www.danielschnyder.com/sheet_music/list_of_compositions_2001.pdf): Recording is cited in composer index; audio was not auditioned.

**Collection and audience context.** The score preview inspected is the soprano saxophone and piano original, Edition Kunzelmann GM1725A. The University of Miami DMA guide concerns the subsequent bassoon adaptation; its bassoon-specific multiphonics and other technique findings are not evidence about the soprano saxophone edition.

**Review focus.** Inference from the inspected opening page: assess coordination of the flowing, slurred soprano line against a rhythmically active piano part; a full score review is needed before judging later movements or the range.

**Open questions.**
- Only the first page of the soprano saxophone score was visually inspected; later movements, overall range, and complete technical demands remain unknown.
- The University of Miami dissertation is a guide to the bassoon adaptation and cannot establish soprano-saxophone performance demands.
