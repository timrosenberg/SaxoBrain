Exact purchase edition verified as Roncorp RON1069, edited by Bruce Ronkin, for soprano saxophone and piano. Existing instruments omit piano. The Brain distributor’s linked TPN1069 preview confirms the Roncorp score title and B-flat soprano staff. Its 1987 copyright is edition metadata, not composition. Académie de Musique de Genève’s 2021 saxophone syllabus lists Fantaisie op.89, Roncorp, in its second-cycle pieces. The syllabus gives a four-year approximate second cycle; this is a school course context, not Tim’s college curriculum. Osaka College of Music’s 2023 research bulletin records Fantaisie op.89 in a saxophone master’s performance repertoire with a thesis on Singelée as a saxophone composer. This documents graduate use alongside the school syllabus. HeBu reproduces Roncorp-series marketing describing original nineteenth-century saxophone/piano contest pieces for high-school and college players. This is broad series copy; it does not prove simplification of Op.89. A Dynamic commercial recording, 4:36, is verified from the official Naxos-delivered YouTube track metadata naming Singelée, Bruno Canino and Sassofoni Accademia Quartet. The ensemble name is the album artist attribution, not evidence this solo is played as a quartet.

Score observations: Inspected the complete one-page Roncorp N1069 public preview, plus opening score pages 2 and 3 of Paul Wehage’s distinct Musik Fabrik edition via its public Sheet Music Plus images. Observations are separated by edition. Roncorp: common time with one flat on the written B-flat soprano staff and three flats in piano. Allegro piano flourish precedes a soprano entry marked Récit. The entry uses accidentals, expressive accents and rising/falling register motion. Roncorp’s m.5 and m.11 areas contain long chromatic sixteenth-note groups interrupted by high fermatas, followed by descending runs. The solo line later moves to più lento with sustained/slurred melody. Piano texture shifts between accented chordal attacks with running treble figures and sparse, separated chords under recitative. Inference: players must coordinate fermatas and flexible timing, rather than maintain an uninterrupted metronomic accompaniment. Wehage’s opening preview explicitly marks the piano recitativo, colla parte, reinforcing the flexible ensemble character in that edition. It is not used to claim identical Ronkin editorial markings. No full range or complete-form analysis is claimed.

Collection context: Original saxophone/piano contest repertoire in a modern Ronkin edition. Documented school second-cycle and graduate performance use; no simplified version established.

Open questions: Ronkin full score, total written range and later sections remain uninspected. Composition year not established independently. IMSLP lists 1863, publication scholarship lists 1864, and Wehage product copy says 1862; no date filled from conflicting event/publication evidence. The current soprano-only instrumentation omits piano; correction requires review rather than silent overwrite.
