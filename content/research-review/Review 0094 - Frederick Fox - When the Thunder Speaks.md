Catalog: [[Frederick Fox - When the Thunder Speaks]]

- Identity: Frederick Fox's *When the Thunder Speaks* (1998) is an approximately 13-minute, one-movement work for alto saxophone and piano, commissioned by Thomas Liley and published by Dorn. A University of North Texas dissertation analyzes the work and reports extended saxophone and piano techniques; its author found no recording at the time of writing.
- UNT dissertation gives alto saxophone and piano, approximate duration 13 minutes, a Thomas Liley commission, one-movement sectional structure organized primarily by tempo, no known recordings, and work-specific technique information: timbral trill (mm. 32-34), growling (mm. 32-34), pitch bending (m. 145), slap tonguing (m. 249); the piano uses fingertip glissandos, palms on strings, and muted keys. Pages 69-70 explain the saxophone effects and the source says the piece uses standard notation. [University of North Texas doctoral dissertation](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc9751/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) (printed pp. 67-70; opened).
- Indiana University Jacobs School of Music lists *When the Thunder Speaks* (1998) among Fox’s solo works and gives publisher listings including Dorn. [Indiana University Jacobs School of Music composer page](https://blogs.iu.edu/jsomcomposition/frederick-fox/) (search_index_only).
- Score link: [Public doctoral dissertation includes analytical discussion of the work; score plates/analysis pages should be consulted.](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc9751/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) (notation not inspected).
- Collection/use: Commissioned by saxophonist Thomas Liley. The UNT author reports no known recording at the time of the dissertation and describes this as Fox's final composition.
- Review focus: The dissertation identifies timbral trill, growling, pitch bending, and slap tonguing in the saxophone part, alongside fingertip glissandos, palms on strings, and muted keys in the piano. These documented effects offer concrete score-review points; the dissertation reports standard notation and no non-conventional rhythmic notation.
- Gaps: The dissertation excerpts include publisher-permission score examples, but no full saxophone score was inspected; exact written range remains unknown.; Recording status may have changed since the dissertation; no recording was located in this pass.

## Chat batch 09 follow-up, 2026-10-13

Gregory E. Miller’s 2008 University of North Texas dissertation gives approximately 13 minutes and a Thomas Liley commission. Miller identifies one movement, organized in sections mainly by tempo, and standard rhythmic notation. His appendix maps an introduction, five main sections, and coda across 288 measures. The dissertation documents fingertip glissandos, palms on strings and muted keys in the piano, alongside timbral trill, growling, pitch bending and slap tonguing in the saxophone. Miller reports no recording in 2008. That historical statement does not establish current recording absence. Indiana University’s composer page independently lists the work under 1998; Groth’s opened exact edition gives alto saxophone, piano, Dorn and DORN1398.

Score observations: Visually inspected dissertation printed pp. 67–69. Its range diagram gives written B-flat3 to C-sharp7, including substantial altissimo. This is an analyst’s diagram rather than an inspection of the complete score. Examples 55–58 explicitly label the saxophone part in written pitch. Example 55 shows a timbral trill on a tied tone, glissando and diminuendo to ppp; example 56 combines chromatic quick notes with a growled sustained ending. Example 57 at m. 145 shows a downward pitch bend with a diminuendo from mf to p. Example 58 at m. 249 shows a descending chromatic run followed by a slap attack at sfz. Only permission-cleared excerpts and the range diagram were inspected; the full 288-measure saxophone part and piano coordination were unavailable.

Collection context: Original commissioned contemporary saxophone work. No young-player simplification documented.

Open questions: No exact accessible playable recording located in current targeted searches. Full saxophone and piano score not inspected; excerpts do not establish every coordination, rhythmic or endurance demand.
