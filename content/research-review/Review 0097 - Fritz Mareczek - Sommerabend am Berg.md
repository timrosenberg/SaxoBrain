## Chat batch 09 follow-up, 2026-10-13

Opened Groth exact edition confirms that ZM11840 includes separate English-horn and alto-saxophone parts plus piano score. These are alternate solo instruments, not a duo. The score preview names English horn or E-flat alto saxophone and piano and prints a playing-time indication of 4,25 Min. A documented historical saxophone recording is listed at 4:25. June Emerson’s opened ZM11840 listing independently confirms cor anglais/alto saxophone and piano. KNMO’s 2010 official wind solo repertoire roster includes this title, Mareczek, alto saxophone and Zimmermann under its own 3e Divisie category. This is an external contest classification, not a SaxoBrain study recommendation. The Finnish Saxophone Society’s own release page confirms Historical Recordings Vol. 2 (2010) includes Eero Linnala’s 1957 recordings. Jazzrytmit’s opened review identifies his Sommerabend am Berg by Mareczek, track 15, 4:25.

Score observations: Visually inspected the exact ZM11840 public first-page preview, marked Klavier. A large diagonal black mask obscures much of the lower page. This is the piano score with a solo cue staff, not the separately printed alto part. The visible piano introduction is Andante rubato, with rolled chords, quick scalar decoration, accidentals, tremolo, sf, accelerando and a diminuendo. The following solo cue enters p and dolce with sustained/slurred notes and short sixteenth-note connections. Later visible upper systems contain more solo-cue sixteenths and chromatic inflections. The keyboard supplies chordal support and independent inner movement; poco accelerando and later ritardando appear. The cue staff’s sounding transposition is not explicitly labeled in the preview. Its pitches cannot be treated as the written E-flat alto part. No complete saxophone range or extended-technique requirement can be established.

Collection context: Published alternate alto-saxophone/English-horn solo parts in one piano edition. No explicit young-player simplification documented. Original orchestral version is a lead; its 1955 composition date was not verified in an opened primary composer/score source.

Open questions: Separate alto part and complete score unavailable; the piano preview’s cue transposition cannot establish alto written range. Historical saxophone recording identified, but no accessible exact saxophone playback verified. English-horn recordings were excluded from saxophone streaming metadata. Original composition date unknown. Presto’s 1956 date is publication, not composition.
