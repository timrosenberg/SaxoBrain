**Identity and findings.** Rumania (1979) is one of three early jazz-inspired works composed or arranged for saxophone by David Heath; a 2020 DMA dissertation provides a performer’s guide and analysis to Rumania alongside Out of the Cool and Coltrane. A contemporary 1988 review describes the work as one movement with contrasting lyrical/rhapsodic and quicker jazz-influenced sections and notes a soprano-sax part was separately available. The original David Heath work is for violin and piano; the saxophone edition is an arrangement, so distinguish the exact sax version from later retailer listings.

## Chat batch 08 follow-up, 2026-10-13

Wise Music dates the original work to 1979 and lists violin [flute] with piano. Written for Nigel Kennedy, it was first performed with John Lenehan at Wigmore Hall on 10 December 1982. Heath’s publisher program note identifies one movement formed from contrasting episodes. The slow sections should allow rhythmic freedom and personal interpretation. Specific piano pedalling produces sustained/blurred sonorities essential to the style. Miles Davis, McCoy Tyner, Ysaÿe and Bartók are named influences. Lindsey O’Connor’s 2020 DMA dissertation explicitly identifies Rumania as composer-arranged saxophone repertoire. Its abstract establishes pedagogical/analytical scope; the full PDF returned 403 and was not read. The 2026 Trinity saxophone diploma repertoire list explicitly includes Rumania for soprano or tenor, Chester. This verifies an accepted tenor alternative but is not a separate-edition finding or a SaxoBrain level assignment. Heath’s own YouTube upload identifies John Harle on soprano and John Lenehan on piano. It is an exact public 10:09 recording.

Collection context: Composer-arranged concert work with documented diploma repertoire use; no simplified youth version established.

Open questions: No actual saxophone notation inspected; written range, articulations and specific fingering demands unknown. Full dissertation unavailable; only its abstract used. Exact saxophone adaptation date remains unknown.
