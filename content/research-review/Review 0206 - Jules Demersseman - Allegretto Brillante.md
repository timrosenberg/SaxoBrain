## Chat batch 12 follow-up, 2026-10-14

The primary Hal Leonard listing identifies Rubank 04476941, publisher code RUBX183, with Himie Voxman as arranger. Groth confirms that this edition includes the piano score and separate solo part. The full two-page Voxman E-flat alto solo part is publicly posted in Kittatinny Regional High School’s Concert Band Sax Proficiencies packet. Its printed copyright is 1940, which dates the edition rather than Demersseman’s original composition. ABRSM’s woodwind syllabus from 2022 lists the Voxman edition for its Grade 7 saxophone exam. This is a documented external exam placement and does not assign a SaxoBrain study year. Dr. Sarah Dunbar explicitly recorded the Voxman arrangement for Kerr Middle School in Burleson, Texas at tempo 108. That is direct youth teaching evidence. No score simplification is claimed by the upload. The Conservatorio Francesco Morlacchi in Perugia lists Allegretto Brillante Op. 46 among recommended saxophone-and-piano repertoire for admission to its first-level academic course. Patrick Pecho’s upload identifies it on a CEU Conservatory graduation recital in 2012.

Score observations: Inspected the entire two-page E-flat alto part on PDF pages 24–25 of the school packet. The second page is upside down in the packet and was rotated for inspection. No piano notation was present in these two pages. The solo is in common time with no initial key signature. It opens with eight bars of rests before accented sustained notes and slurred sixteenth-note runs. Triplets and a trill appear in the opening phrase. Short dolce passages use slower values and hairpin shaping between much denser sixteenth-note writing. The running passages contain frequent chromatic accidentals, mixed slurred and detached groups, and repeated articulations. The visible part reaches written high F on several occasions and descends into the lower register. The ending accelerates through Più animato to Presto, then descends through a slurred scale before separated final notes. The part requires contrast between lyrical shaping and fast fingerwork, exact handling of accidentals, and the ability to alternate slurred and tongued groups. The piano coordination cannot be assessed from the solo-only packet.

Collection context: Standalone work.

Open questions: The original instrumentation and original composition year were not established. Voxman is explicitly credited as arranger, so an original-for-alto claim is withheld. Publisher Closer Look returned unavailable. The inspected school packet contains the complete solo part but no piano score. Youth teaching use is verified; no evidence that Voxman’s edition specifically simplifies the original was located.
