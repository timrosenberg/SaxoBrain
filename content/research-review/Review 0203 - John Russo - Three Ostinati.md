## Chat batch 12 follow-up, 2026-10-14

The opened DORN0192 dealer description identifies John Russo and the alternative clarinet or alto saxophone scoring. The public opening score confirms those alternatives and prints 1964 next to the composer name. The first page has no arranger credit. This is direct work-specific evidence of Russo’s composition for the stated alternative solo instruments. Lawrence Gwozdz’s uploaded Southern Illinois University guest recital of February 27, 1996 lists the complete three-movement work beginning at 48:38: Moderately fast, Blues style, and Rather fast. The following work begins at 56:33, so that chapter occupies about 7:55 including the interval before the next selection. This recital documents university guest-artist use. No matching youth syllabus, simplified edition or beginning-player teaching purpose was located.

Score observations: Inspected only the opening piano-score page shown as the dealer product image. It is labelled for clarinet or alto saxophone, and the solo staff is labelled in C. The printed footnote says E-flat alto saxophone transposes an octave lower; this preview is not the transposed solo part. The opening is in common time at quarter note = 120. An eighth-note piano bass figure repeats below the solo entrance, which starts after rests with a grace figure, accented sustained notes and hairpin dynamics. The visible solo line includes chromatic accidentals, slurred motion and tied or held notes over the recurring bass. Later in this page the piano right hand takes up continuous eighth-note activity. Coordination with the moving accompaniment and contrast between accented sustained notes and slurred motion are visible demands. No later movement notation, complete range or special-technique inventory was inspected.

Collection context: Standalone work.
