## Chat batch 11 follow-up, 2026-10-14

Leduc’s Ibert catalog identifies the 1949 original for oboe and string orchestra, duration 27 minutes. It lists Nicole’s soprano-and-piano transcription as AL29268. The 1951 date on the edition is distinct from the original’s completion year. Groth’s live exact-edition record and Hal Leonard’s official product page independently identify soprano saxophone and piano. Actual AL29268 soprano-part preview explicitly names Nicole and the soprano scoring. Wise Music’s work record calls the scoring alto saxophone and piano. This conflicts with the actual score part and exact-edition distributor record. Do not add alto or alter the target soprano identity from that label. The Styrian wind-music association’s GOLD examination repertoire lists Ibert/Nicole Symphonie Concertante for soprano and piano, originally oboe and orchestra. Cyprus’s 2025–2026 music-school performance competition lists the complete work in soprano repertoire. These document teaching/examination contexts; no simplified young-player edition was verified. Only first-page piano and soprano notation previews were inspected. They support concrete demands in the opening movement; they do not establish the whole work’s range or later-movement techniques. Orchestra currently in the SaxoBrain fields is not supported by this soprano-and-piano publication.

Score observations: Exact AL29268 first soprano-part page: treble clef, common time, Allegro con moto quarter note 138. It begins with long counted rests and rehearsal/cue references before chromatic slurred lines and register changes. The same soprano preview has repeated-note and moving sixteenth-note writing, sixteenth-note triplet groups, rallentando followed by Tempo, and dynamics ranging from pp to ff. These observations concern written B-flat soprano notation, not concert pitch. The AL29268 piano score’s first printed page is headed for oboe and orchestra and has an instrument line. It shows an active chromatic reduction, repeated bass figures, triplets, chordal figures, clef changes and dynamic contrasts. This is not evidence of an authorized saxophone-orchestra version. Teaching inference from the two opening previews: prepare chromatic fingering under slurs, precise entry after counted rests, and coordinated tempo/dynamic transitions with the pianist. Full-work endurance and range require the remaining score.

Collection context: Oboe concerto transcription for soprano and piano. Music-school competition and wind-association examination use documented. No explicit young-player simplification; saxophone college/professional performance not located.
