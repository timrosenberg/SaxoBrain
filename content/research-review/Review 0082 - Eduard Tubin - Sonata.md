## Research

Catalog: [[Eduard Tubin - Sonata]]

- Identity: Eduard Tubin completed his Sonata for alto saxophone and piano in Stockholm on 9 March 1951. The BIS CD 269 booklet documents its first performance in Uppsala on 23 September 1951 by Jules de Vries and Martin Keil, and the recorded performance by Pekka Savijoki and Roland Pöntinen. The three movements are timed 5:59, 4:41, and 3:24.
- Booklet gives completion date, first performance and performers, and identifies the three movements and track timings. [BIS CD 269 booklet](https://eclassical.textalk.se/shop/art28/BIS-CD-269_booklet_scan.pdf-cd34f8.pdf) (PDF pp. 1-3; opened).
- Program note describes a first movement with saxophone and piano themes, climax and virtuoso saxophone runs; the Adagio follows without a break and draws on a 13th-century troubadour song, while the finale is fast and concludes with a virtuoso rush. [BIS CD 269 booklet](https://eclassical.textalk.se/shop/art28/BIS-CD-269_booklet_scan.pdf-cd34f8.pdf) (PDF p. 3; opened).
- Recording: [BIS CD 269 performance by Pekka Savijoki, saxophone, and Roland Pöntinen, piano; movements listed as 5:59, 4:41, and 3:24.](https://eclassical.textalk.se/shop/art28/BIS-CD-269_booklet_scan.pdf-cd34f8.pdf) Booklet inspected; playback not auditioned.
- Collection/use: Original alto saxophone sonata with documented 1951 premiere and a commercial recording.
- Review focus: Inference from the booklet's program note: check the first movement's virtuoso saxophone runs, the attacca into the Adagio, and the fast finale's climactic rush. These details are program-note descriptions, not notation observations.
- Gaps: No score or notation preview located; range, articulation, and other note-level demands remain unknown.

## Chat batch 08 follow-up, 2026-10-13

Freshly downloaded BIS-CD-269 booklet identifies the original alto saxophone recording by Pekka Savijoki and Roland Pöntinen, with movements Allegro, Adagio and Allegro vivace. Its timings are 5:59, 4:41 and 3:24, total 14:07. Harri Kiisk’s booklet note describes development of the saxophone’s initial theme against the piano’s dance music. Virtuosic saxophone runs lead without a break into the Adagio, based on a thirteenth-century troubadour song. The middle section moves faster and recalls the Sixth Symphony’s tenor-saxophone motif. The finale exchanges a second theme between piano and saxophone, broadens at the climax and concludes with a virtuoso rush. Eino Tubin’s 2017 biography says the slow movement began as a freestanding cello piece, Troubadour Song from the 13th Century. It also reports Tobias Lund’s recognition of a middle-movement motif from Judy Garland’s On the Bumpy Road to Love and the composer’s manuscript permission to perform on viola. The two freshly read primary sources disagree on the first complete performance day: BIS says September 23, 1951; Eino Tubin’s biography says September 22. Both give Jules de Vries and Martin Keil in Uppsala. The discrepancy should remain visible. June Emerson’s exact NMS1998 record confirms alto saxophone or viola with piano, Gehrmans, issue 1993 and ISMN M661541998. Issue 1993 is not the original composition year. Virgo Veldi and Johan Randvere’s full video is supplied from Randvere’s channel and linked by the Tubin Society. The caption identifies a 2015 live recording in Tubin Hall, Tartu and gives all three movement starts. Browser playback was verified.

Score observations: Inspected the BIS booklet’s actual Musical Examples page 18. Roman-numbered examples I–VI correspond to the sonata discussion. These are short thematic extracts, not a complete score or saxophone part. Example I shows a connected solo melody with slurs and dynamic hairpins. Example II shows the contrasting piano theme in two-staff texture. The Adagio and finale extracts also display contrasting rhythmic profiles. Instrument transposition is not explicit in these isolated examples, so no written saxophone range is stated.

Collection context: Original three-movement saxophone sonata. Composer-authorized viola alternative is documented. No youth simplification or school-edition context verified.

Open questions: Only six thematic excerpts were inspected. The complete saxophone part, complete written range, exact high-register demands and later technical notation remain unverified. Premiere day differs by one day between BIS booklet and Eino Tubin biography. No single precise day is asserted. The Adagio’s thirteenth-century-song description and Judy Garland motif attribution come from different primary accounts. Both are attributed rather than flattened into a single origin claim.
