Québec lists it among simple lyrical pieces for sound development at general level 3 and again at 4/5. ### Research Review, October 11, 2026

[[Review 0244 - Marcel Dautremer - Reverie Interrompue|Full review note]]

Catalog: [[Marcel Dautremer - Reverie Interrompue]]

- Identity: The Philharmonie de Paris guide describes Dautremer’s Rêverie interrompue as a complete 1954 work for alto saxophone and piano, in two connected 4/4 sections, first slow and peaceful and then faster, with both slurred and detached articulation. It lists duration as four minutes. Windmusic’s library record adds AL21352, score and part pagination, and a pedagogical repertory year descriptor.
- Guide gives 1954 date, two connected 4/4 parts, contrast, articulation and complete-piece performance context. [Philharmonie de Paris, 10 ans avec le saxophone](https://catalogue.philharmoniedeparis.fr/AloesSynchro/stockage/10-ans-avec-le-saxophone.pdf) (PDF p.40, printed p.43; opened).
- Windmusic library record confirms Leduc AL21352, 1954, 4-page score plus part, four-minute duration and its own pedagogical descriptor. [Windmusic library](https://windmusic.org/index.php?id=11413&lvl=notice_display) (search_index_only).
- Quebec conservatory syllabus places the work among simple, songful repertoire selections for tone study. [Conservatoire de musique et d’art dramatique du Québec](https://www.conservatoire.gouv.qc.ca/media/0oulvph0/cmadq-progexternes-saxophone.pdf) (search_index_only).
- Score link: [Pedagogical guide, PDF page 40 / printed page 43](https://catalogue.philharmoniedeparis.fr/AloesSynchro/stockage/10-ans-avec-le-saxophone.pdf) (notation not inspected).
- Collection/use: Pedagogical repertoire with documented conservatory syllabus use; not enough evidence to claim a simplified transcription.
- Review focus: Inference: the documented shift from slow, peaceful writing to a faster section with mixed slurred and detached articulation makes phrasing and contrast concrete review points.
- Gaps: No printed score notation inspected.; The guide describes “justesse et musicalité” but does not specify written/sounding pitch range.
