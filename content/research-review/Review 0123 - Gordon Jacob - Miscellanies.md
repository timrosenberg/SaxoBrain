## Chat batch 09 follow-up, 2026-10-13

The official composer work page states Date written: 26 February 1976 and describes alto saxophone/wind band, also arranged for alto/piano. The seven movements are Scalic Prelude, Folk Song, Moto Perpetuo, Interlude, Gavotte, Dirge and Quick March. Emerson’s exact E66 catalogue confirms alto saxophone/piano and ISMN M570400690. Its D skill label is the retailer’s own approximate ABRSM Grades 6–7 category and is not a SaxoBrain level. A competing historical account by Philip Scowcroft says the piano work was written for a World Saxophone Congress premiere by Paul Harvey and later scored for strings. This conflicts with the official work page’s version history. Use the official composer page for metadata and retain this discrepancy for further primary-score/premiere research.

Score observations: Public Emerson E66 pages 2 and 3 display single-staff treble-clef saxophone notation, with the product identifying the alto/piano edition. Written pitches are transposed alto-part pitches. Only these excerpts were viewed. Folk Song begins Moderato con moto, quarter note = 108, in common time. The preview shows p cantabile, lyrical slurs, dynamic hairpins, trills, triplets, short ornamental figures and a six-bar rest. Teaching focus inferred from this excerpt: sustained melodic direction and clear transition between plain and ornamented lines. Moto Perpetuo begins Allegro molto, quasi presto, quarter note approximately 114–120, in 3/4. After a three-bar rest, flowing sixteenths run under long slurs, with accidentals, register shifts and f to ff contrasts. Teaching focus inferred from this excerpt: even legato fingers, pulse stability and controlled breaths through long rapid phrases. The excerpts do not show piano notation or all seven movements. Complete written range, ensemble difficulty and presence or absence of extended techniques are unknown.

Collection context: Seven-movement concert suite with contrasting character pieces. Official instrumentation supports wind-band performance and a piano edition. The rapid Moto Perpetuo excerpt provides substantive technique evidence; no source identifies a young-player simplification.

Open questions: Original-versus-arranged scoring history conflicts between official work page and Scowcroft account. Only saxophone-part pages 2–3 inspected; no full score or piano part. No exact public playable performance verified.
